Two or more screens

Here’s what you’ll make. One video file that plays across two or more screens. In Premiere you make one HD sequence for each screen, then put them side by side in one wide sequence and export it as one file. The lab’s array has three screens, so the examples here use three.

Why we’re doing this. The computer treats the screens as one wide display, but in the room they’re separate screens with gaps between them. Making each screen’s video on its own means each one fits its screen exactly.

What you’ll come away with. How to build a sequence as wide as all your screens, place one video on each screen, and play the result full screen across the array.

What students have made with three screens

  1. 2024

    Marotetini Tehei, Disparate Diaspora. Marotetini put three versions of herself on the three screens: Tahitian, French and Australian. Each one argued from its own screen, in its own language. The work ended with the three of them reaching across the gaps between the screens in a physical struggle.

    The steps below keep each loop inside its own screen, because anything that crosses a gap breaks. In Disparate Diaspora the personas cross the gaps on purpose, when the argument turns into a physical struggle.

Part 1Assemble your screens in Premiere

You’ll make one 1920 × 1080 sequence for each screen, then put them side by side in one wide master sequence. Bring the videos you want on the screens: loops from the After Effects workshop, footage you shot, or something from the other step-by-steps.

T1 · Make one sequence per screen 4 steps

  1. T-01

    Open Premiere Pro and make a new project, ticking Skip import mode in the New Project dialog. In the Project panel, double-click where it says to import media (or choose File → Import) and bring in your videos.

  2. T-02

    Choose File → New → Sequence. On the Settings tab, set Editing Mode to Custom, Frame Size to 1920 × 1080, Pixel Aspect Ratio to Square Pixels, and Timebase to match your videos (30 for phone video and your After Effects loops). Name it Screen 1.

  3. T-03

    Give it a white background. In the Project panel, click the New Item icon at the bottom and choose Color Matte. Click OK on the size (it already matches your sequence), pick white, and name it. Drag it onto V1, then drag its end out to the full length of the sequence.

    Why a Color Matte? It’s exactly the size of the frame, with nothing to line up, and it doesn’t fade in or out. Premiere has no background colour setting for a sequence: anything empty exports as black.
  4. T-04

    In the Project panel, select Screen 1 and choose Edit → Duplicate once for each other screen. Rename the copies Screen 2, Screen 3 (right-click, or Control-click, and choose Rename).

T2 · Fill each screen 1 step

  1. T-05

    Double-click Screen 1 to open it. Drag its video onto V2, above the white background. Click the video in the Program Monitor, then drag it into place and drag a corner handle to size it. Do the same in Screen 2 and Screen 3.

    Keep each video inside its own screen. The gap between the screens isn’t in the file, so anything that crosses it would jump sideways when it reached the next screen.

T3 · Put the screens side by side 2 steps

  1. T-06

    Choose File → New → Sequence again: Custom, Square Pixels, the same Timebase as your screens, height 1080, and width 1920 times the number of screens. Name it Master.

    2 screenswidth 3840
    3 screenswidth 5760
    4 screenswidth 7680
    Note: the video misses this. In the video, the Master’s Timebase is left at the default, 23.976. Don’t copy that: set it to the same Timebase as your screens (30 for phone video and your After Effects loops), or Premiere has to drop frames to fit them in.
    Why multiply by 1920? Each screen is an HD screen, 1920 pixels wide, and the file lays them side by side. If the screens are 4K, they scale the HD picture up to fill themselves.
  2. T-07

    Turn on snapping: click the spanner icon at the bottom right of the Program Monitor and choose Snap in Program Monitor. Drag Screen 1, Screen 2 and Screen 3 from the Project panel into the Master sequence, each on its own track. Then select each one in the timeline and drag it in the Program Monitor until they snap edge to edge, left to right. To make it exact, set each one’s Position in the Properties panel: 960, 2880, 4800 across (and 540 down).

    Asked about a clip mismatch? Choose Keep existing settings, so the Master stays as wide as your screens.
    Where do 960, 2880 and 4800 come from? Position is the centre. Each screen is 1920 wide, so the centres are 1920 apart, starting at 960 (half of 1920). A fourth screen would be at 6720.
  3. ✓

    Check before you go on. The three screens should sit side by side, edge to edge, filling the wide frame with no gaps or overlaps.

Part 2Export and play on the screens

E1 · Export, then play full screen in VLC 2 steps

  1. T-08

    Choose File → Export → Media, set Format to H.264, and export.

  2. T-09

    On the computer connected to the screens, open the file in VLC. Choose Playback → Repeat, then Video → Fullscreen.

  3. ✓

    Check that it works. You should see each video on its own screen, playing round.
