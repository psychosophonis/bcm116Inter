Panorama

This is the low bar. Drop a Hockney collage into Premiere and you've got a 360 world you can stand inside on the Quests. That's all a panorama is: an image twice as wide as it is tall, wrapped around the viewer.

Once that works, mess with it. Add green screen video. Alter it in Photoshop. Make your own panoramic photograph. Every one of these ends up as the same 2:1 file going through the same Premiere steps.

Here's what you'll make: drag to look around

Facing straight ahead

David Hockney, Pearblossom Highway, 11–18th April 1986 (#2), placed twice on a 6000 × 3000 canvas. In the headset you just turn your head.

Part 1Hockney into Premiere

Download the Hockney panorama (6000 × 3000). If you've got a soundtrack, bring that too.

P1 · Make it a 360° video 7 steps

  1. P-01

    Open Premiere Pro and make a new project. In the New Project dialog, tick Skip import mode and click Create. In the Project panel, double-click where it says to import media (or choose File → Import) and bring in the Hockney panorama and your soundtrack, if you have one.

  2. P-02

    Choose File → New → Sequence. On the Settings tab, set Editing Mode to Custom, Frame Size to 6000 × 3000 and Pixel Aspect Ratio to Square Pixels. Then click the VR Video tab and set Projection to Equirectangular and Layout to Monoscopic.

    Why 6000 × 3000? All panorama is 2:1. Across is turning all the way round; up and down is looking from your feet to the sky.
  3. P-03

    Drag the image into the sequence. Drag it out to the length you want (2 mins).

    This image is already the right size, so dragging it onto the New Item button would also make a sequence for you. Building the sequence yourself means you can make your own 6000 × 3000 images and drop them in, or fill the sequence with any content you like.
  4. P-04

    Add your soundtrack to the audio track if you have one.

    You don’t need sound, but it lets you make interesting combinations of panoramic image and sound.
  5. P-05

    To preview in 360°, click the spanner icon at the bottom right of the Program Monitor and choose VR Video → Enable. Drag with the mouse to look around.

  6. P-06

    Go to File → Export → Media. Set Format to H.264. Click Video; you might have to fold down the More options. At the bottom of the pane, tick Video is VR and make sure Frame Layout is Monoscopic. Then export.

    Forget to tick Video is VR and YouTube plays it flat. Nothing changes in the picture. The tick writes a mark into the file that tells YouTube it's 360°.
  7. P-07

    Upload to YouTube and test it in a headset. Open the YouTube app on a Quest, find your video and turn around.

  8. ✓

    Check it works. In a browser, drag across the YouTube video and the view should turn. If it plays flat, give YouTube time to finish processing before you export again.

Part 2Mess with it

The panorama is just an image, so anything you can put on an image you can put in the world around the viewer.

P2 · Add video, alter the image 5 steps

  1. P-08

    Put some green screen video on the track above the panorama. If you haven’t shot any, Pixabay has green screen clips you can use for free.

  2. P-09

    Choose Window → Effects. In the Effects panel, open Video Effects, then the Keying folder, and find Ultra Key at the bottom of the list. Drag Ultra Key onto the green screen clip in the timeline.

  3. P-10

    Find the Effect Controls panel, select the Key Color option, and use the eyedropper to pick the green background of the clip. It should disappear.

  4. P-11

    Select the clip in the timeline again. Drag it around and scale it so it sits on the horizon, halfway up the frame. This is easier with VR preview off.

    Keep it near the middle. The top and bottom of the frame get stretched when they wrap round your head. The horizon is where things look the right shape.
  5. P-12

    Open the panorama in Photoshop and change it: paint on it, cut things out, collage in your own photos. Keep it 6000 × 3000 and export it as a PNG. Back in Premiere, right-click (or Control-click) the image in the Project panel and choose Replace Footage.

Part 3Make your own panoramic photo

Use a DSLR on a tripod. Choose a location that's panoramic and has depth: lens distortion shows most in the near field, so small rooms are harder to stitch.

In reality this is a good way to see how a panoramic image is assembled, rather than a way to build a project. Manual tweaking will nearly always be needed.

These are basic instructions. Ask Mat to run you through the process himself: it will be a lot clearer than stumbling through it. The main thing to understand is that a panorama is just an image that’s 2:1 (6000 by 3000 pixels), plus some metadata that Premiere writes into the file when you tick Video is VR (P-06). So you can make a panorama from text, images, drawings, whatever.

P3 · Shoot, stitch, make it 2:1 4 steps

  1. P-13

    Shoot in manual, including manual focus. Set the aperture to f/22 (or f/16 if you need to) for deep focus, the shutter to 1/60 or faster, and the ISO so the exposure meter reads dead centre. Set the focal length to 24 mm and hold the camera upright. Turn the camera on the tripod and shoot overlapping images all the way round: about 15 on the Canon 90D.

    Why go wide? Fewer images means fewer seams for Photomerge to get wrong, and a wider lens covers more of the view above and below the horizon, so there’s more to look at in the headset. The cost is more distortion, especially on things close to the camera, so keep near objects out of the shot.
  2. P-14

    Make smaller copies of your photos before you stitch them. In Photoshop, choose File → Scripts → Image Processor. Click Select Folder… and pick your folder of photos. Tick Save as JPEG with Quality 8, tick Resize to Fit and set W and H to 2000 px. Run it.

    Resize to Fit keeps each photo’s shape: only its longer side becomes 2000 pixels, so an upright photo comes out 1333 × 2000.

    Why make them smaller? Full-size camera files are huge. Stitched at full size they come out with weird artefacts, and the lab machines struggle. 2000 pixels is still plenty for a 6000 × 3000 panorama.
  3. P-15

    Go to File → Automate → Photomerge. Choose Layout Auto, tick the options that let Photoshop manipulate your images, select your smaller copies and click OK. Fix the seams with the Clone Stamp or Healing Brush, then merge your layers without the background (Control-click a layer and choose Merge Visible).

  4. P-16

    Start a new document that's 6000 × 3000. Copy the merged layer into it (⌘C, ⌘V). Press ⌘T and scale it so the panorama wraps as seamlessly as possible from the far left to the far right. Export it as a PNG, then take it through P-01 to P-07.
