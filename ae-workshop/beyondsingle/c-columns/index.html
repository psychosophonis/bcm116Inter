Multipanel on one screen

Here’s what you’ll make. An HD video for one screen with several shots playing at once, side by side in a row or stacked in a grid.

Why we’re doing this. Put shots next to each other and they start talking to each other: the same action in different places, or the same shot starting at different moments. The screen becomes one picture made of many.

What you’ll come away with. How to scale, place and time a stack of clips in Premiere: drag them into place, then tidy them up with numbers.

What students have made

  1. 2025

    Walk this way, a tutorial exercise. Everyone in the group filmed twenty seconds of their own feet walking on a surface they were drawn to. They held their phones at waist height, with their feet at the bottom of the frame. Five walks run side by side as strips, and the surfaces keep changing: tactile paving, timber, brick, sand and water.

    Every shot was framed the same way, with the phone at the waist and the feet at the bottom of the frame. That’s why five different walks still read as one picture.

Part 1Assemble your shots in Premiere

Shoot or find several clips, or use one clip several times. Shoot them the same way, like Walk this way, and different shots still read as one picture. The numbers below are for phone video at HD (1080 × 1920 upright, 1920 × 1080 sideways). The steps are in Premiere, but the same numbers work in any editor that lets you type position and scale.

C1 · Set up and bring your shots in 2 steps

  1. C-01

    Open Premiere Pro and make a new project. In the New Project dialog, tick Skip import mode and click Create. In the Project panel, double-click where it says to import media (or choose File → Import) and bring in your clips.

  2. C-02

    Choose File → New → Sequence. On the Settings tab, set Editing Mode to Custom, Frame Size to 1920 × 1080 and Pixel Aspect Ratio to Square Pixels.

    Why HD rather than 4K? The screens scale HD up to fill themselves, so you don’t need to work at 4K.

C2 · Drag, drop and scale 1 step

  1. C-03

    Drag each clip into the sequence on its own track, stacked up: the first on V1, the next on V2, and so on. If you run out of tracks, drop the clip in the empty space above the top one and Premiere adds a track.

    Then click a clip in the Program Monitor. Drag it around, and drag one of its corner handles to make it smaller or bigger. This is how to arrange anything by eye. The next two parts give you exact numbers for a clean layout.

C3 · Columns: five upright clips side by side 2 steps

  1. C-04

    Turn on snapping: click the spanner icon at the bottom right of the Program Monitor and choose Snap in Program Monitor. Now clips snap to the edges of the screen and to each other as you drag them.

    Select the first clip. Open Effect Controls. Click the small arrow to the left of Motion to show its settings, and type Scale 35.6. Drag the clip to the left edge of the screen; to make it exact, type Position 192, 540 there too.

    Use Effect Controls for the numbers. The Properties panel rounds Scale to whole numbers; Effect Controls keeps the decimal.
    Where does 35.6% come from? The screen is 1920 wide, so five columns are 1920 ÷ 5 = 384 wide each. Upright phone video is 1080 wide, so the Scale is 384 ÷ 1080 × 100 = 35.6%. On a calculator: 1920 ÷ 5 ÷ 1080 × 100. Want a different number of columns? Change the 5. Shot in 4K? Use 2160 instead of 1080.
  2. C-05

    Select the second clip, type the same Scale in Effect Controls, and drag it so its left edge meets the first clip’s right edge. To make it exact, type Position 576, 540.

    Keep going: each clip goes 384 further right. The third is at 960, the fourth at 1344 and the fifth at 1728.

    Shot sideways? A sideways clip scaled to 384 wide is only 216 tall. To make tall strips from sideways clips, leave them at full size and crop instead: under Crop, the first clip gets Right 80%, the second Left 20% and Right 60%, and so on, Left going up by 20 and Right going down by 20.

C4 · Grid: two rows of upright clips 2 steps

  1. C-06

    For a grid instead, select the first clip. In Effect Controls → Motion, type Scale 28.1 and drag it to the top-left corner. To make it exact, type Position 200, 270.

    Where does 28.1% come from? Two rows means each clip is half the screen’s height: 1080 ÷ 2 = 540 tall. Upright phone video is 1920 tall, so the Scale is 540 ÷ 1920 × 100 = 28.1%. On a calculator: 1080 ÷ 2 ÷ 1920 × 100. Want three rows? Change the 2. Each clip comes out 304 wide, so six fit across.
  2. C-07

    Select the second clip, type the same Scale in Effect Controls, and drag it so its left edge meets the first clip’s right edge. To make it exact, type Position 504, 270.

    Keep going: each clip goes 304 further right, six across: 808, 1112, 1416, 1720. Then do the second row the same way at a height of 810.

    Clip lands in the wrong place when you type its Position? Its Anchor Point has moved, which happens if you drag the small crosshair in the middle of the clip instead of the clip itself. In Effect Controls, set Anchor Point back to 540, 960, the centre of an upright clip.
    Shot sideways? Use three rows of three instead: Scale 33.3% (1080 ÷ 3 ÷ 1080 × 100), with centres at 320, 960, 1600 across and 180, 540, 900 down.

C5 · Stagger and repeat them 1 step

  1. C-08

    Stagger the clips so they don’t all start together. On each track except the first, drag the clip’s left edge to the right by a different amount (1 second on the second track, 2 on the third, and so on), then drag the clip back to the start of the sequence.

    Then make each clip run longer by repeating it. Select it, press ⌘C, press ↓ to jump to the end of the clip, and press ⌘V to paste a copy straight after. Keep pressing ↓ then ⌘V until it’s as long as you want. Trim the ends so all the tracks finish together.

    Why stagger them? Every copy jumps back to the start of the clip. If all the tracks jump at the same moment, the whole screen jumps at once. Staggered, each panel restarts at its own time and the cuts ripple across the screen.
    Pasting onto the wrong track? ⌘V pastes onto the highlighted track (the track name on the left of the timeline). Click the clip’s own track name first.
    One clip, many times. Use the same clip in every panel and start each one a little later than the last. A pattern moves across the screen that no single copy has on its own.
  2. ✓

    Check before you go on. Press the spacebar and watch it as one picture.

E1 · Export as H.264 1 step

  1. C-09
