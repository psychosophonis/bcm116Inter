Remix time and space

Here’s what you’ll make. A video for one screen, made from one shot. You cut the shot into five vertical strips with masks and start each strip a couple of seconds later than the one beside it. Anything that moves across the frame breaks where the strips meet.

Why we’re doing this. It’s a simple way to start working with time inside a single image. Artists like Daniel Crooks take this a long way further; this is a method for getting started.

What you’ll come away with. How to cut a layer into strips with masks, how to start each strip at a different moment, and five more things to try: speed, zoom, rings, slow rotation and turning against the motion.

This card is useful for prompt 3 (camera movement, Daniel Crooks) and prompt 9 (procedural generation / repetition). Want several shots side by side instead? See Multipanel on one screen.

Part 1Cut one shot into strips

You need one video clip, at least 20 seconds long: footage you shot, a clip from Pixabay (free to use), or a loop you made in the After Effects workshop. It works best with the camera on a tripod and something moving across the frame, like people walking or cars passing. Pick something different from the people around you, so you can compare results.

R1 · Fill the screen with your shot 5 steps

  1. R-01

    Open After Effects and make a new project: click New Project on the start screen, or choose File → New → New Project. Import your shot, then drag it from the Project panel onto the big New Composition button in the empty Composition panel.

    Why make the composition from the clip? It gets the clip’s own frame rate and length, so nothing stutters and nothing is cut short.
  2. R-02

    Choose Composition → Composition Settings. Type 1920 as the width. Most video is 16:9, so with Lock Aspect Ratio ticked the height becomes 1080 by itself. If it doesn’t, untick Lock Aspect Ratio and type 1080.

  3. R-03

    In the Project panel, right-click (or Control-click) your shot and choose Interpret Footage → Main. Under Other Options, set Loop to 2 times.

    What does this do? Interpret Footage is where After Effects keeps its settings for how to read a file. Loop 2 times makes it treat your shot as if it were joined to itself end to end, so it’s twice as long. In Part 2 you’ll slide each strip so it starts partway into the shot; without the loop, a strip that starts 8 seconds in would run out 8 seconds before the end and go black.
  4. R-04

    Select the shot in the timeline and choose Layer → Transform → Fit to Comp Width. It fills the screen from side to side, whatever size it started.

    A square loop sticks out top and bottom. That’s fine: only what’s inside the frame shows.
  5. R-05

    Choose Layer → Pre-compose, pick Move all attributes into the new composition, untick Open New Composition, and click OK.

    Why pre-compose? Your shot is now inside a layer exactly the size of the screen, 1920 × 1080, whatever you started with. So the mask numbers below work for everyone.
  6. ✓

    Make sure you’re in your main composition. Look at the top left of the Composition window, just under its tab. You’ll see your compositions named in a row. Your main composition is the one on the far left: click its name. You’re in the right place when the timeline shows the pre-comp as a single layer.

R2 · Cut it into five vertical strips 3 steps

  1. R-06

    With the layer selected, pick the Rectangle tool and drag a tall box over the left fifth of the picture. The box becomes a mask. Then tidy it up: choose Layer → Mask → Mask Shape, set Units to Pixels, and type Top 0, Bottom 1080, Left 0, Right 384.

    Check that the layer is selected first. If nothing is selected, the Rectangle tool makes a new shape layer instead of a mask.
    Box bigger than the picture? That’s fine: anything outside the frame doesn’t show. Mask Shape tidies it to the frame. 1920 ÷ 5 is 384, so each strip is 384 pixels wide.
  2. R-07

    Click the layer’s name in the timeline once, so the whole layer is selected rather than its mask. A double-click opens the pre-comp instead, so check you’re still in your main composition: its name is on the far left of the bar at the top left of the Composition window. Then choose Edit → Duplicate (or press ⌘D) until you have 5 layers.

    Got a Mask 2 instead of a new layer? The mask was still selected, so After Effects copied the mask. Press ⌘Z to undo, click the layer’s name, and duplicate again.
  3. R-08

    Select the second layer and choose Layer → Mask → Mask Shape. Change only Left and Right: Left 384, Right 384*2. Then do the third layer: Left 384*2, Right 384*3. Keep going until the fifth layer’s Right is 384*5, the full 1920.

    You can type sums. After Effects works out anything you type into a number box: 384*3, or 768+384, both give 1152. Each strip starts where the last one ended, so its Left is the last one’s Right.
  4. ✓

    Check before you go on. The whole picture should be back, with no gaps or overlaps between the strips.

Part 2Start each strip at a different moment

R3 · Offset the strips in time 1 step

  1. R-09

    Drag each strip’s bar left in the timeline so it starts a little further into the shot than the strip to its left. Then tidy it up: right-click (or Control-click) the heading Source Name, above the names of your layers on the left of the timeline, and choose Columns → In. Then type In times from left to right: 0:00:00:00, -0:00:02:00, -0:00:04:00, -0:00:06:00, -0:00:08:00.

    It won’t loop perfectly. Each strip jumps back to the start of its shot at a different moment. A hard cut is fine.
  2. ✓

    Check before you go on. Press the spacebar. Anything moving should break where the strips meet.

R4 · Set the loop where every strip is playing 1 step

  1. R-10

    Find the stretch where every strip has footage in it, and make that the whole composition.

    Move the time indicator to where the last strip’s bar starts and press B. Move it to where the first strip’s bar ends and press N. Then choose Composition → Trim Comp to Work Area.

    What are B and N doing? The work area is the light grey bar along the top of the timeline: it’s the part of the composition After Effects plays and exports. B sets where the work area begins, N sets where it ends, each at the time indicator. Trim Comp to Work Area then cuts the composition down to just that part.
    Why? At the start and end, some strips have nothing in them yet, so the picture pops up one strip at a time. Trimmed to the stretch where they’re all running, your loop plays with every strip full, all the way round.
  2. ✓

    Check before you go on. Play it from the start. Every strip should have footage in it the whole way through.

R5 · Play with it 1 step

  1. R-11

    Now change things and watch what happens. Try one at a time:

    • Mix up the order. Swap the In times between strips, so the delays don’t step evenly from left to right.
    • Try different offsets. Bigger gaps (4 seconds apart), smaller ones (half a second), or uneven ones.
    • Try different sized strips. Change the Left and Right numbers in Mask Shape so some strips are narrow and some are wide. Each strip’s Left is still the last one’s Right, and the last Right is still 1920.
    Changed the offsets? The stretch where every strip is playing moves too. Do R-10 again before you export.

Part 4Ways to remix

These start from the pieces you just made: strips here, rings in some, but try them on a grid or any shape you like. Each one works for a different reason, and each needs the right kind of shot.

R6 · Give a strip its own wrapper, for zoom and rotation 1 step

  1. R-13

    All five strips share the pre-comp from R-05, so changing anything inside it changes every strip. To zoom or turn one strip on its own:

    1. In your main composition, click one strip’s layer name once.
    2. Choose Layer → Pre-compose, pick Leave all attributes, and click OK.
    3. Double-click the new layer to go inside it. Select the one layer in there and change its Scale or Rotation.
    4. Click your main composition’s name, on the far left of the bar at the top left of the Composition window, to go back. Repeat for the next strip.
    Strip changed size or moved? The Pre-compose dialog remembers your last choice, Move all attributes. Undo, and pick Leave all attributes.

R7 · Rings instead of strips 3 steps

  1. R-14

    In the Project panel, select your main composition and choose Edit → Duplicate (or press ⌘D). Double-click the copy to open it. It has your five layers and their offsets already in it.

  2. R-15

    Turn each strip into a circle. Select a layer, choose Layer → Mask → Mask Shape, tick Reset To and choose Ellipse. Then type its box. Every circle is centred on the middle of the frame (960, 540), so for a circle of radius r the box is Left 960-r, Right 960+r, Top 540-r, Bottom 540+r. Type the sums straight in.

    Layer 1, the centrer = 135
    Layer 2r = 270
    Layer 3r = 405
    Layers 4 and 5r = 540
    Why those numbers? 540 is half the height of the frame, so the biggest circle just touches the top and bottom. Each ring is 135 wider than the last.
  3. R-16

    Cut the hole in each ring. On layer 2, choose Layer → Mask → New Mask, then open Mask Shape for the new mask, choose Ellipse and give it the radius of the ring inside it (135). In the timeline, open the layer’s Masks with the small arrow and change the new mask from Add to Subtract. Do the same on layer 3 (hole 270) and layer 4 (hole 405).

    Layer 5 is everything outside the rings: change its only mask from Add to Subtract.

    Add and Subtract. An Add mask shows what’s inside it. A Subtract mask cuts away what’s inside it. A big Add circle with a smaller Subtract circle in the middle leaves a ring.
  4. ✓

    Check before you go on. The whole picture should be back, as a centre circle, three rings and the outside, with no gaps. Press the spacebar: each ring should be at a different moment. For rotation, give each ring its own wrapper as in R-13.

R8 · Change the speed and turn the rings 2 steps

  1. R-17

    To change a strip’s speed, select it in your main composition and choose Layer → Time → Time Stretch. Set the Stretch Factor: 200% plays it at half speed, 50% at double speed. Give each strip its own speed and they drift in and out of step.

    Keep to real time or slower. Fast walking looks comical. If a slow strip looks jerky, choose Layer → Frame Blending → Pixel Motion.
  2. R-18

    To turn a ring, give it its own wrapper (R-13) and go inside. Select the layer in there. Move the time indicator to the start and click the stopwatch next to Rotation in the Properties panel. Move the time indicator to the end and set Rotation to 1x+0°: one full turn. Do the same on the other rings with different amounts, some turning the other way (-1x).

    How fast is that? One full turn over the whole composition. In a 30-second composition that’s 360 ÷ 30 = 12° a second. Keep it slow: fast spinning gets in the way of what’s in the footage. Scale the layer up a little so its corners never show.

Now shoot your own. Content matters: what goes in is what comes out. Look at what made your test shot work, and at what worked for the people around you, then go and find that. Most of these have a camera on a tripod, strong lines and colour, and something moving across the frame.
